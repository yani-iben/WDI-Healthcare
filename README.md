The well-being of a nation’s citizens is closely tied to the way its government allocates resources, especially in critical sectors like education and healthcare. With countries spending a significant portion of their annual budgets on these areas, it’s important to explore how these investments actually impact people’s lives. This project is driven by one overarching question: How does government spending affect citizens’ quality of life?  We aim to analyze the relationship between healthcare spending and the survival rate of a country’s population. If survival improves with more spending, that would support the case for increased public investment in health. By answering this question, we hope to provide meaningful insights into how healthcare budgets influence key social and economic outcomes. These insights could help inform government priorities and show how smart investment strategies can lead to real improvements in people’s lives.


The WDI: Education, Health & Employment (2011-2021) dataset is found through Kaggle where the dataset has been compiled and exported directly from the World Bank’s official World Development Indicators database covering the years from 2011 to 2021. It contains socioeconomic indicators for 19 advanced economies: Australia, Austria, Belgium, Canada, Denmark, Finland, France, Germany, Italy, Japan, Republic of Korea, Netherlands, Norway, Spain, Sweden, Switzerland, United Arab Emirates, United Kingdom, and the United States. This dataset contains indicators that details 14 main aspects, with 95 columns and 210 rows. Education expenditure refers to the financial allocation dedicated to primary, secondary, tertiary, and overall public education institutions. Educational attainment represents the proportion of the adult population achieving various education levels, ranging from primary education to doctoral degrees, with distinctions by gender.
